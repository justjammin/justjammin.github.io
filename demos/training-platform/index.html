01 / ACTUAL EXECUTION

Inspect the run.

What happens when a training job crashes halfway through? This recording follows a small model learning to sort banking questions into 77 categories. First it trains without interruption. Then a second run is deliberately stopped at update 7 and restarted from the saved checkpoint at update 5.

The test: can the restarted run finish all 20 updates and reach the same training state as the uninterrupted run? The final comparison passed.

How to explore: The terminal starts with the full transcript. Choose a numbered chapter to inspect one stage, or select Play recording to watch the captured output appear in order. Pause to read, change the playback speed, or choose Show all to restore the complete transcript. This replays saved output; it does not launch a new training job.

How to read the terminal
  • Timestamps and commands: Brackets show elapsed recording time and the stage name. Lines beginning with $ are commands; the lines below are their recorded results.
  • The failure is intentional: state: failed, exit 1; expected 1 and Driver exited -9 show the planned worker shutdown. committed_step: 5 identifies the last complete saved checkpoint.
  • Recovery reuses saved work: The resume command restores checkpoint 5 and repeats updates 6–7 before continuing to 20. That is why there are 22 raw metric rows but 20 canonical rows: the raw log keeps both attempts, while the canonical view counts each update once.
  • Verification checks more than completion: The comparison covers model weights, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, random-number state and progress through the data. final_step: 20 and the final “Full-state recovery comparison passed” message show that recovery completed and matched the reference within the stated tolerance.
  • Evaluation measures a different question: Macro-F1 scores how well the model classifies unseen questions, giving each of the 77 categories equal weight. Higher is better, up to 1. These low scores come from a tiny, 20-update baseline; the recovery result demonstrates training reliability, not a production-ready classifier.

Recorded 2026-09-17 on macOS / arm64, Python 3.12.14. This replay uses a local CPU; the separate T4 LoRA experiment appears in GPU LoRA below.

Deployment scope and scaling roadmap

The verified deployment combines single-host CPU/Ray workers, single-GPU LoRA jobs and Databricks reporting. The architecture separates compute, checkpoint state and reporting through versioned artifacts. Production expansion would add durable shared checkpoint storage, managed job orchestration, access controls, monitoring and workload-specific release gates, then validate multi-node throughput and host-loss recovery under load. Verified configuration, recovery and reporting contracts provide the foundation for that expansion.
